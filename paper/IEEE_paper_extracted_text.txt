   Experimental results show that our approach performs
remarkably well compared to the extractive and zero-shot
baselines, in terms of all evaluation metrics considered. The
results obtained by ROUGE and METEOR metrics show great
performance in terms of content coverage and naturalness
respectively. The excellent performance in BERTScore reflects
that the language is semantic well-aligned. Moreover, the
improvements in NER Preservation and MEDCON scores
demonstrate better tracking of clinical concepts and named
entities.
   These results prove the significance of adapting to the
clinical domain for clinical text summarization tasks, where
better summarization results can be achieved. The produced
summaries can be used directly in the clinical environment
because they will be structured and more meaningful. In
summary, this approach can provide a reliable way for sum-
marizing medical text documents.
   Future Work: Several directions can be used to solve
identified problems. First, generalization capabilities of our
model can be enhanced via training on a larger set of data and
using additional clinical documents. Second, the performance
can be further evaluated by employing bigger test datasets and
performing expert review. Third, additional information from
other documents, such as lab results and locked EHRs, can
be added to achieve better context-awareness of summaries.
Fourth, we can perform model compression or knowledge
distillation to speed up inference process.
